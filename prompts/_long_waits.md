LONG WAITS (#1042) — never sleep-poll a wait longer than about 4 minutes inside your session. Your prompt cache lives 5 minutes: a foreground `sleep` loop that wakes after 9-10 minutes rewrites your whole context as a cache write (150K-300K tokens, about $1 per wake on Sonnet, against about $0.08 for a cache read), and that pattern was 72% of two days' spend. Not allowed, whatever the Bash timeout: `for i in $(seq 1 58); do …; sleep 10; done`, `timeout 585 bash -c "until …"`, `end=$((SECONDS+570)); while …`. A job that may run longer than about 4 minutes (a suite, a gate, a bench batch, a Windows-host run, a build): start it with Bash `run_in_background`, write its output to a log, then end your turn or do other work. Its exit wakes you, and waiting costs nothing. Check it once on that wake; never loop on it. A wait on another lane (a lock queue, a merge, a PR) is not a job you can background: park the lane where a park exists (a lock queue, #1034) instead of polling. Polling you cannot avoid: one short check per wake, each wake under 270 seconds so the cache stays warm. Never a blocking loop.
