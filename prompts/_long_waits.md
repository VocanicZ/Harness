LONG WAITS (#1042, #1066) — never wait inside your session; the engine waits for you. Your prompt cache lives 5 minutes: a foreground `sleep` loop that wakes after 9-10 minutes rewrites your whole context as a cache write (150K-300K tokens, about $1 per wake on Sonnet, against about $0.08 for a cache read), and that pattern was 72% of three days' spend. A job that may run longer than about 4 minutes (a suite, a gate, a bench batch, a Windows-host run, a build): start it with `harness-wait run --deadline-s <most seconds it may take> --log <logfile> -- <command…>`, then END YOUR TURN at once: one short reply naming what you wait for (no promise, no more tool calls). `harness-wait run` starts the command detached, keeps its output in the log and its exit code in `<logfile>.rc`, and writes your wait spec; the engine checks it for you at no model cost and resumes this session ONCE, when the job exits or the deadline passes, with `wait done: <condition>, rc=<n>, log tail: …`. Judge the result from that message and read the log for more; never loop on it. Other conditions: `harness-wait set --deadline-s <s> --pid <pid>` (a process exits), `--path <file>` (a file appears), `--regex <re> --log <file>` (a line appears in a log from now on). Not allowed, whatever the Bash timeout: `for i in $(seq 1 58); do …; sleep 10; done`, `timeout 585 bash -c "until …"`, `end=$((SECONDS+570)); while …`, or a Bash `run_in_background` you then keep polling. A wait on another lane (a lock queue, a merge, a PR) is not a job you can run: park the lane where a park exists (a lock queue, #1034) instead of polling. A usage limit needs nothing from you: the engine parks the lane and resumes it once the limit has cleared. If you park yourself on a limit or a reset time (`harness-wait set --limit --until <epoch>`), omit `--deadline-s`: the deadline resumes you too, and `waitlib` refuses one that ends before `--until` (#1100). A wait that ends HOURS from now (more than 2 hours: a bench or budget block waiting for a usage window to reset, `harness-wait set --limit --until <epoch>`, or `--path <file> --deadline-s <s>`) PARKS you (#1154): commit and push first, then run it; it posts your handoff, swaps `agent-working` for the `waiting-quota` label and ends your session, so your pool slot goes to the next ready issue, and the engine re-dispatches the issue as a resume once the wait is over — do nothing more after it prints `PARKED`. A refusal (unpushed work) leaves your session as it was; `--resume inject` keeps your slot, and is for waits you really must hold. Never sit on a pool slot for hours. Polling you cannot avoid: one short check per wake, each wake under 270 seconds so the cache stays warm. Never a blocking loop. SUB-AGENTS (#1249) — your own sub-agents are not jobs you can wait on, and each one's notification is a wake after the 5-minute cache expired (140K-350K tokens of cache write each; four bangwa lanes were parked for it). Sub-agents always run in the background here. Start every sub-agent of one fan-out in ONE message, so they start together and tend to finish together. Every sub-agent brief tells it to run every command in the foreground: no `run_in_background`, `Monitor` or `harness-wait run` inside a sub-agent, and never end its turn before its final report. Each sub-agent then notifies you exactly once; one that backgrounds its work and ends its turn sends an interim "completed" you pay a wake for, and a second when it really finishes. When a sub-agent's notification arrives while other sub-agents are still running, make no tool calls: write one short line and end your turn (no re-reading files, no `git status`, no check on the others). Never poll a sub-agent (`TaskOutput`, reading its output file, or `sleep`).
